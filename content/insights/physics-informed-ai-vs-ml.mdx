# Why Physics-Informed AI Beats Pure ML for Industrial Predictive Maintenance

Predictive maintenance sounds straightforward: collect sensor data, train a machine-learning model, predict failures before they happen. And it works — right up until it doesn't.

The failure mode of pure ML in industrial settings is well-documented but rarely discussed in vendor literature: when operating conditions shift outside the training distribution, models extrapolate arbitrarily and catastrophically. A compressor running at a new duty point, a furnace operating on a different fuel blend, a motor driving a modified load — all of these scenarios can produce confident but wrong predictions from a purely statistical model.

## What Physics-Informed Neural Networks Do Differently

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) embed governing equations — heat transfer, fluid dynamics, mechanical vibration — directly into the loss function during training. The network is penalised for predictions that violate physics, not just predictions that differ from labelled training data.

The result: extrapolation that respects physical law, even for operating regimes never seen during training.
